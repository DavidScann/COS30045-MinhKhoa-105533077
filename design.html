Data Visualisation Design Report

An analysis of external data visualisations communicating the size of prison populations, drawing on sources such as Pew Research, World Population Review, and the Prison Policy Initiative.

1. Metrocosm Prison Population Map

Source: metrocosm.com (Archived)

Purpose: To show the geographical distribution and scale of the US prison population.

Kind of chart: Thematic Map (Proportional symbol map).

Encoding: Uses area/size of symbols or color intensity to encode the number of prisoners per region/state.

Interactive features: Often includes tooltips on hover to show exact numbers, and sometimes a time slider.

Effectiveness: Strong visual contrast and geographical context to highlight regions with disproportionately high incarceration.

2. Pew Research Line Chart

Source: Pew Research Center

Purpose: To illustrate the trend of the US incarceration rate over time, specifically showing its decline since 1995.

Kind of chart: Line chart.

Encoding: X-axis encodes time (years), Y-axis encodes the incarceration rate (per 100k people).

Interactive features: Typically static, though may have hover effects for specific data points.

Effectiveness: Clear communication of temporal trends. The downward slope effectively communicates the "lowest since 1995" narrative.

3. World Population Review: Global Rates

Source: World Population Review

Purpose: To compare incarceration rates globally.

Kind of chart: Interactive Choropleth Map / Data Table.

Encoding: Color saturation on the map encodes the incarceration rate per 100,000 population.

Interactive features: Hoverable tooltips on map regions reveal the exact country rates. Searchable and sortable data tables below.

Effectiveness: Extremely effective for exploratory analysis, allowing users to benchmark specific countries against global trends.

4. Prison Policy Initiative (2024)

Source: Prison Policy Initiative

Purpose: Highlighting how US states compare to independent countries regarding incarceration.

Kind of chart: Horizontal Bar Chart.

Encoding: Length of bar encodes the incarceration rate. Distinct colors separate US states from international nations.

Interactive features: Often static in reports, occasionally providing tooltips.

Effectiveness: Highly persuasive. By interleaving US states with countries, it contextualizes state-level policies on a global scale.

Data Insight:

Based on the 2024 data from the Prison Policy Initiative, the United States continues to incarcerate more people per capita than any other nation, with rates often exceeding 500 per 100,000 residents in states like Louisiana and Mississippi, compared to nations like the UK or Canada which hover around 130-140 per 100,000.
