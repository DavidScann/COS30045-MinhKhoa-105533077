Exercise 7.1 Data Visualisation Design

Comparing and contrasting different visualisation designs communicating the size of the prison population.

Visualisation 1: Metrocosm Prison Population Map

Purpose: To show the geographical distribution and scale of the US prison population over time or space.

Kind of chart: Thematic Map (likely proportional symbol map or choropleth).

Encoding: Uses area/size of symbols or color intensity to encode the number of prisoners per region/state.

Interactive features: Often includes tooltips on hover to show exact numbers, and sometimes a time slider.

Features to get message across: Strong visual contrast and geographical context to highlight regions with disproportionately high incarceration.

Visualisation 2: Pew Research Line Chart

Purpose: To illustrate the trend of the US incarceration rate over time, specifically showing its decline since 1995.

Kind of chart: Line chart.

Encoding: X-axis encodes time (years), Y-axis encodes the incarceration rate (per 100k people).

Interactive features: Typically static, though may have hover effects for specific data points.

Features to get message across: Clear trend line, annotations, and a clear title summarizing the finding.

Visualisation 3: World Population Review

Purpose: To compare incarceration rates globally across different countries.

Kind of chart: Choropleth Map and Data Table/Bar Chart.

Encoding: Color saturation on the map encodes the rate. Bar length in the chart encodes the rate.

Interactive features: Hovering over a country displays its specific statistics.

Features to get message across: Global context, highlighting the US's position relative to the rest of the world using striking colors.

Visualisation 4: Prison Policy Initiative (Global Comparison)

Purpose: To starkly demonstrate how individual US states compare to entire countries in terms of incarceration.

Kind of chart: Horizontal Bar Chart.

Encoding: Length of the bar encodes the incarceration rate. Color distinguishes US states from other countries.

Interactive features: Usually static or simple tooltips.

Features to get message across: Sorting values descending and mixing US states with sovereign nations to emphasize the extreme scale of US incarceration.

Visualisation 5: Prison Policy Initiative (Detailed)

Purpose: To break down the global context further, possibly by specific demographics or facility types.

Kind of chart: Stacked Bar Chart or specialized Bar Chart.

Encoding: Length and color segments encode different categories of incarcerated individuals.

Interactive features: Static or limited interactions.

Features to get message across: Detailed categorization showing exactly where the numbers come from (e.g., local jails vs state prisons).

Visualisation 6: Prison Policy Initiative (Women)

Purpose: To highlight the specific statistics regarding women's incarceration.

Kind of chart: Pie Chart / "Whole Pie" structural breakdown.

Encoding: Area/Angle encodes the proportion of women in different types of correctional facilities.

Interactive features: Mostly static with detailed labeling.

Features to get message across: Visualizing the "whole" system and dissecting it, using colors and text annotations to make the sub-populations instantly readable.

Conclusion: Best Design

Which design is best and why? Visualisation 4 (Prison Policy Initiative horizontal bar chart) is often the most impactful. By interleaving US states with independent countries, it shatters the assumption that the US as a whole is just a single outlier. It effectively uses length (the most accurate visual variable for comparison) and color (to highlight US vs. non-US) to tell a compelling, immediate story about the scale of the issue.
