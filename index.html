Home

Welcome to the Appliance Energy insight dashboard. Use this interactive platform to explore energy factors like standby consumption, appliance size, and efficiency labels in the Australian market.

In Australia, televisions and household appliances are significant contributors to residential electricity use; choices such as screen size, display technology, and usage hours directly affect annual energy consumption.

Energy Consumption for different TV screen types and sizes

Across 3,703 Australian TV models, annual energy use ranges from as low as 56 kWh up to 2,652 kWh — a 47-fold difference. The majority of sets cluster below 600 kWh, but large-screen and older-technology models push well beyond that. Use the buttons to see how each screen technology contributes to that spread.

Star ratings are not a reliable guide to running costs on their own. A 1-star TV can use anywhere from under 200 kWh to over 1,500 kWh depending on screen size, and even 6-star models span a wide range. Screen technology and physical size are the two strongest predictors of how much electricity a TV actually draws — hover over any dot to see the details.

LCD panels are by far the most energy-efficient technology on average, at 297 kWh/year. LED sets average 458 kWh — 54% higher — while OLED screens average 506 kWh, the highest of the three. For a household running a TV 6 hours a day, choosing LCD over OLED could save around $70–$90 per year at current electricity rates.

Australian electricity prices have risen from 8.5c/kWh in 1998 to 34.2c/kWh in 2024 — a four-fold increase in 26 years. Every kWh an inefficient TV wastes now costs four times as much as it did in the late 1990s. A TV that uses 600 kWh/year instead of 300 kWh/year now costs an extra $103 annually, compared to just $21 in 1998.
